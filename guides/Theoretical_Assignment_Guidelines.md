# General Guidelines for Theoretical Assignments and Diagnostic Assessments

These guidelines apply to all theoretical assignments and diagnostic assessments. Each student is required to comply with them, and compliance is taken into account in grading. Solving exercises is the most effective way to consolidate your understanding of the course material; devote the necessary time to them and do not leave the assignment until the last minute.

---

## 1. Presentation and Formatting

Each solution must be **handwritten and legible**. “Handwritten” means either writing on paper or writing digitally with a stylus on a tablet; **typed text does not satisfy this requirement**. Solutions that do not meet this requirement will receive a grade of zero for the corresponding exercise.

The following formatting requirements must be observed:

- The top of the first page must include the student’s full name, Student ID number, assignment number, and date. Page numbers must appear in the bottom-right footer.
- Exercises must be solved in the order in which they appear in the assignment instructions, and each exercise must be completed in full before the next one begins. Example of an unacceptable practice: beginning Exercise 2 on page 3 and continuing Exercise 1 on page 5. Such cross-references between pages, as well as solutions presented in an order different from that of the assignment instructions, will not be assessed.
- If an exercise is not answered, the following must be written in its place: “Exercise X has not been solved.”
- Sufficient space must be left between exercises and steps so that they are visually distinct and there is room for the grader’s comments.
- The pages of the submission must be scanned in the correct order (page 1 first, and so on).

Failure to comply with the formatting rules above will result in a grading penalty, weighted as specified in Section 3.

---

## 2. Assignment Submission

The assignment must be submitted electronically and exclusively through Blackboard as **a single PDF file**, named according to the pattern `StudentIDNumber_Surname_AssignmentTitle.pdf` (e.g., `UC1234567_Ioannou_Assignment1.pdf`), by the stated deadline.

- Solutions may be handwritten directly with a stylus in a tablet application and exported as a single PDF file.
- Solutions handwritten on paper must be converted into a single PDF file using a scanner or scanning application (e.g., [Adobe Scan](https://acrobat.adobe.com/us/en/mobile/scanner-app.html)), with adequate lighting and high image quality.
- After submission, each student must confirm that the file opens correctly, that the pages are legible, and that they appear in the correct order.

**Late submissions are not accepted.** An exception may be considered only in a documented emergency, such as a serious medical incident. The student must notify the instructor before the deadline or, if this is objectively impossible, as soon as possible after the incident, and must provide the required supporting documentation. In an approved, documented emergency, the instructor will determine the method and timing of an alternative assessment, particularly if model answers have already been posted.

After the deadline has passed, model answers will be posted, and no further extension or modification of the submitted work will be permitted. Submission outside the terms stated above, or failure to comply with the required submission format (e.g., multiple files instead of a single PDF), will result in a grade of zero.

---

## 3. Solution Quality and Assessment

Each solution must meet the following measurable criteria:

- **Correctness**: the final result is correct.
- **Completeness**: all essential intermediate steps are presented. Where required by the nature of the exercise, the applicable inference rule, axiom, definition, theorem, or method must be stated explicitly. Omitting essential steps or justification will result in a lower grade, even if the final result is correct.
- **Clarity**: any nonstandard, new, or specifically defined notation must be explained when it is first used. Established notation taught in the course may be used without restating its definition. The sequence of steps must be linear and easy to follow, without logical gaps. A step that is not explicitly connected to the preceding one is considered incomplete, regardless of whether the result is correct.
- **Consistency**: the reasoning leads logically to the stated final result.

The grade for each exercise is determined by combining the criteria above with compliance with the formatting rules (Section 1) and submission rules (Section 2), using the following **indicative** weights:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Correctness of the result | 40% |
| Completeness of steps/justification | 30% |
| Clarity and legibility | 20% |
| Compliance with formatting/submission requirements | 10% |

A partial solution that is clear and fully justified may receive a higher grade than a solution that includes a final result or numerous steps but lacks clear and sufficient justification. Any questions about the assignment instructions must be resolved in a timely manner, before the deadline, in consultation with the instructors.

## 4. Academic Integrity, Use of Sources, and AI Tools

The use of books, notes, and other sources to understand concepts is acceptable. A section titled “Sources and Use of AI Tools” must be added at the end of the assignment’s final page. For every external source or AI tool, students must state the source or tool, the part of the assignment in which it was used, and how it contributed.

**Permitted**: the use of AI tools exclusively to understand concepts (e.g., to explain a definition), provided that the final solution is entirely the student’s own work and the use of the tool is explicitly disclosed. For example, students may discuss the general approach to an exercise with a fellow student (e.g., “Which theorem is applicable here?”) or use an AI tool to explain a definition or concept.

**Not permitted**: using AI tools to generate, correct, or reproduce a final solution; copying assignments from fellow students or other sources. For example, students may not share or copy the final text of a solution, nor may they ask an AI tool to solve the exercise, produce a proof, supply essential steps, or rephrase the final answer for submission, even if they have studied it or are able to explain it.

Both the student who copies and the student who provides work to be copied are subject to the same consequences. Each student bears individual responsibility for the answers submitted in their name. Disclosing the use of AI does not in itself make that use permissible; such use must always be consistent with the course’s learning objectives.

Students must also comply with the [University of Cyprus Artificial Intelligence Guidelines](https://www.ucy.ac.cy/ai/policy/ucy-guidelines/).

Any violation of the academic integrity rules in this section, including copying and the prohibited use of AI tools, will result in a grade of zero for the assignment and may lead to referral to the Disciplinary Board or to other applicable sanctions.

---

## 5. Delays, Complaints, and Communication

- Questions about the assignment instructions must be submitted in a timely manner, before the submission deadline.
- Requests for grade review must be submitted within 5 working days of the publication of grades, with a written justification identifying the specific point of disagreement.
